## Evaluation

The major discrepancy is the conversion of identifications into quantitative features and supported protein ratios. It is not explained by a simple shortage of identified PSMs. Search/digestion/database differences deserve documentation and a controlled comparison, but the new measurements put MS1 detection, mapping and quantitative selection ahead of a wholesale search rerun.

Three findings refine the earlier assessment:

1. **Identification counts were misleading when compared as rows.** The deposited MaxQuant mzTab has 384,372 PSM rows, including repeated protein-accession rows and 260 decoy rows. It contains approximately 247,500 distinct target PSM identities; NF-SILAC has 272,604. This does not establish superior identification accuracy, but it argues against poor identification yield as the dominant explanation for the protein-ratio coverage gap.
2. **The two-peptide support threshold explains a substantial internal loss, but a small fraction of the publication gap.** With the existing features, allowing one contributing peptide would raise Mix2 coverage from 531–540 to 915–938 groups per injection. The publication has 2,991–3,067 H/M groups per injection. Most of the discrepancy would remain.
3. **The low overall CV is strongly affected by selection.** On 402 identical Mix2 groups quantified in all three injections, published H/L median CV is 2.34%, versus 3.33% here. The unmatched comparison of roughly 8% published versus roughly 3% here does not demonstrate a precision advantage.

The heavy-channel deficit remains substantially shared with published DDA results. Its physical cause is not established. The data support a quantification/selection investigation; they do not justify median-centering the deliberately unequal mixtures or assigning the deficit uniquely to OpenMS.

## Paper and reference evidence

The relevant paper is **Borteçen, Müller and Krijgsveld (2023), “An integrated workflow for quantitative analysis of the newly synthesized proteome,” Nature Communications 14, 8237**, DOI [10.1038/s41467-023-43919-3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-43919-3), [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38086798/). PXD039580 is its HeLa SILAC DDA benchmark, associated with Figure 4; it is separate from the IFNγ time course. The two benchmark mixtures are L:M:H = 70:15:15 and 20:40:40, each with three technical injections. The benchmark cultures were SILAC-labeled for ten days, rather than the short pulse used in other experiments in the paper.

## What differed in the executed analyses

These settings are established from the deposited benchmark XML, the published NF-SILAC config table, recorded workflow/module behavior and executed parameters embedded in consensusXML.

| Component | Deposited MaxQuant benchmark | Completed NF-SILAC benchmark | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Search / rescoring | MaxQuant 2.0.3 / Andromeda | Comet / per-run Percolator | Counts and scores are not interchangeable accuracy measures |
| Digestion | Trypsin/P; two missed cleavages | Trypsin; two missed cleavages | Real metadata difference; before-proline cleavage contribution is small in the exported reference |
| Non-label modifications | Carbamidomethyl(C) fixed; Oxidation(M), protein N-terminal acetylation variable | Same non-label modification set | Deamidation is absent from the deposited benchmark XML and mzTab, despite broader wording elsewhere in the paper |
| Label chemistry | L; Lys4/Arg6; Lys8/Arg10 | Same triplex definition | No evidence here of a gross channel-chemistry or orientation error |
| Precursor search | Initial 20 ppm and main 4.5 ppm search; recalibration configured | Metadata-driven 4.5 ppm; no explicit equivalent recalibration stage in this workflow | Potential search sensitivity difference; counts do not identify it as the principal bottleneck |
| Fragment matching | FTMS 20 ppm | Explicit Comet 0.02 Da setting | Different engine semantics; no universal ppm-to-Da equivalence |
| Protein evidence | Unique and razor peptides; minimum unique peptide count 0 | `protein_quantification=unique_peptides`; cross-group shared peptide ratios excluded | More conservative routing of available quantitative peptides |
| Minimum protein ratio support | `minRatioCount=0` | `ratios:min_ratio_count=2` | One-peptide quantitative groups disappear here |
| Missing-feature recovery | Requantification enabled | No equivalent MaxQuant requantification step | Potentially important for weak channels and ratio coverage |
| Match between runs | Enabled | Disabled | Potentially important for completeness; distinct from requantification and missing-channel detection |
| Spectrum preparation | Vendor RAW input; `useMs1Centroids=false`, `useMs2Centroids=false` in XML | ThermoRawFileParser conversion followed by PeakPickerHiRes before search and MS1 processing | Different feature/peak preparation; inspect preservation of isotope-envelope signal in a diagnostic run |
| MS1 feature eligibility | MaxQuant's own isotope-feature/quantification procedure | FFM: intensity cutoff 1,000; 3–6 isotopes; RT band 0; `knock_out=false` | Strong candidate for the upstream feature-selection gap |
| Labeled residue limit | `maxLabeledAa=3` | `max_nr_labelled_aas=2`, meaning up to three residues | Numerically different parameters with matching effective limits; not a reason to increase the setting blindly |
| Ratio output used here | Published DDA table; raw/normalized source-column provenance still to verify | Stored raw ratios, light reference; H/M derived from H/L and M/L | Preserve estimator/reference distinctions |

The available reviewed human FASTA at the recorded NF-SILAC input path has 20,431 entries. The candidate February 2021 FASTA has 20,394; 20,292 accessions overlap and 19,731 of those have identical sequences. The candidate comes from PXD048162, not a FASTA deposited directly with PXD039580. Its name matches the benchmark XML, but the paper reports 20,934 entries. Neither that entry-count discrepancy nor immutable identity with the original search database is resolved. The historical target-plus-contaminants derivative has 20,775 entries. Correcting that provenance is appropriate for a future reference-matched search. It does not explain a six- to ten-fold quantification coverage difference on its own.

## New identification and quantification measurements

### Comparable identification identities

PSMs were matched by run, native scan, sequence, charge and non-label variable-modification positions/masses. Isotope annotations and fixed carbamidomethylation were removed from the comparison key because the exporters represent them differently. The original MaxQuant output contains 247,494 distinct target PSM IDs; the standardized key yields 247,493 identities. NF-SILAC has 272,604 standardized identities, with 210,447 shared identities across the six runs.

| Run | MaxQuant distinct target PSM keys | NF-SILAC target PSM keys | Shared keys | NF-SILAC identified sequences | Quantified sequences |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Mix1 DDA1 | 40,329 | 44,862 | 35,057 | 28,445 | 2,170 |
| Mix1 DDA2 | 41,689 | 45,217 | 35,552 | 27,296 | 2,111 |
| Mix1 DDA3 | 41,594 | 45,736 | 35,971 | 27,568 | 2,144 |
| Mix2 DDA1 | 41,070 | 44,528 | 33,680 | 22,918 | 3,491 |
| Mix2 DDA2 | 41,522 | 46,238 | 35,205 | 23,458 | 3,422 |
| Mix2 DDA3 | 41,289 | 46,023 | 34,982 | 23,598 | 3,600 |

Quantified sequences here are unmodified sequence strings represented by retained features, with charge and modification variants collapsed, compared with identified sequence strings at the same grain. Retention is 7.63–7.78% in Mix1 and 14.59–15.26% in Mix2. These are different measured populations from protein groups or mzTab peptide rows.

More accepted identifications do not establish better true FDR or search accuracy: target/decoy handling, rescoring, database and export stages differ. Nevertheless, the high overlap and absence of an identification-count deficit make “Comet identified too little” an inadequate explanation of the large ratio-coverage loss.

Only 994 standardized MaxQuant PSM identities end in K/R immediately followed by proline, approximately 0.40% of its target identities; none do in NF-SILAC. This measures one clear Trypsin/P-specific terminal class, not every effect of enzyme specificity. Identified NF-SILAC sequences with more than three K/R residues constitute only about 0.2–0.3% per run. Those checks do not support enzyme or labeled-residue limits as dominant explanations for the current gap.

### Replaying protein support from stored peptide ratios

The new script reconstructs group membership from consensusXML, routes peptide references to compatible inferred groups and reaggregates their stored peptide ratios. It reproduces **all 5,172 exported non-reference protein ratios and their support counts**, with no missing/extra keys and no numerical mismatch at `rel_tol=1e-6, abs_tol=1e-7` (protein arrays use float precision).

Of 6,130 quantified modified peptide identities, 5,446 route to one group and 684 do not under the current group-compatibility rule. The latter 11.2% are excluded from protein-ratio contributions; they are not failed peptide measurements.

| Mixture | Current ≥2-peptide groups/injection | Same features with ≥1 peptide | Published H/L groups/injection | Fraction of mean publication gap recovered by support change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Mix1 | 318–342 | 590–651 | 3,333–3,416 | 9.4% |
| Mix2 | 531–540 | 915–938 | 2,988–3,064 | 15.7% |

Published H/M counts are 3,329–3,414 for Mix1 and 2,991–3,067 for Mix2. Pair-specific publication counts differ, while the retained full-triplex OpenMS population yields equal M/L and H/L support eligibility.

The one-peptide alternative is an offline sensitivity calculation, not a new processing run or proposed default. It neither detects new features nor changes group inference. All ratios have at least one measured contributing peptide, so a zero threshold would add no new evidence-supported ratio beyond this one-peptide calculation. The added groups require their own accuracy, missingness and precision assessment.

Lowering the support requirement alone also preserves the heavy deficit: Mix2 H/L medians remain approximately 1.610–1.635, versus 1.622–1.651 with two-peptide support, against an expectation of 2.

### Precision with population selection controlled

For each ratio, both methods were restricted to exactly the same protein accession sets with measurements in all three injections. CV is sample SD divided by mean on linear ratios. This explicit complete-case comparison differs from summaries permitting two available injections.

| Mixture / ratio | Identical complete-triplicate groups | Published median CV | NF-SILAC median CV |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Mix1 M/L | 231 | 2.48% | 3.28% |
| Mix1 H/L | 231 | 2.43% | 3.00% |
| Mix1 H/M | 231 | 2.96% | 3.66% |
| Mix2 M/L | 402 | 2.35% | 3.38% |
| Mix2 H/L | 402 | 2.34% | 3.33% |
| Mix2 H/M | 402 | 1.84% | 3.31% |

Published complete-case H/L CV is 12.71% on its full Mix1 population and 8.37% on its full Mix2 population. Restriction to our shared groups reduces those medians to 2.43% and 2.34%. Selection is therefore a measured explanation for much of the apparent overall precision advantage. Estimator/raw-normalization differences remain; the result does not isolate their contributions.

## Heavy-channel bias: established evidence and unresolved causes

The earlier exact-group comparison remains informative:

| Matched population | Expected H/L | Published mean-over-injections group median | NF-SILAC equivalent median |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Mix1, 415 groups | 0.2143 | 0.1764 | 0.1850 |
| Mix2, 664 groups | 2 | 1.6413 | 1.6272 |

For Mix1, restricting the publication from all available groups to our shared groups changes its H/L median from 0.2401 to 0.1764, reversing the apparent bias relative to the intended mixture. Population selection matters for accuracy as well as precision. On shared Mix2 groups, both analyses retain an approximately 18% H/L deficit.

The current findings support the following distinctions:

- **Established:** reference-table ratios share a heavy deficit on identical protein-group populations; protein support and feature selection strongly affect which entities survive; exported stored protein ratios can be reproduced from their source peptide ratios.
- **Supported candidate:** full-triplex detection and weak-channel/isotope-pattern gates contribute to coverage differences. Executed settings are `knock_out=false`, 3–6 isotopes, intensity cutoff 1,000 and RT band 0. All 18,661 retained feature/run rows have three positive channel intensities in the existing QPX audit. Mix1 has two 15% channels and substantially lower retention despite more identified sequences. This is consistent with weak-channel selection, but the fraction of rejected candidates attributable to each gate has not been measured.
- **Unresolved physical causes:** mixing/label-incorporation differences, isotope impurities, overlapping envelopes/interference and MS1 extraction/aggregation effects. Ten-day labeling alone does not measure incorporation. Agreement with published ratios does not independently identify the physical cause.
- **Weak evidence for a dominant proline-conversion explanation:** earlier descriptive peptide diagnostics show similar deficits with/without proline and in lysine-only/arginine-only classes. This does not exclude conversion in individual peptides.
- **Insufficient explanation:** low signal alone; earlier retained diagnostics show the deficit persists in high-light-intensity subsets. These are selected, unmatched populations, not a raw-envelope assay of every channel.

H/M reference arithmetic was also checked on the fixed feature population. Evidence-level H/M was calculated from the stored positive channel ratios, then aggregated by median to peptide and group, rather than dividing already aggregated protein H/L and M/L. Mix2 medians are 0.8912–0.8973, compared with 0.8921–0.8949 from division of protein ratios. This is an alternative aggregation using fixed features/inference, not a native reference-2 workflow rerun. The distinction changes individual estimates, but it does not remove the overall heavy deficit.

The [OpenMS ratio documentation](https://openms.de/current_doxygen/html/classOpenMS_1_1MS1LabeledRatioQuantifier.html) supports the evidence → peptide → protein median hierarchy. The [FeatureFinderMultiplex documentation](https://openms.de/documentation/html/TOPP_FeatureFinderMultiplex.html) describes the intensity, isotope, neighboring-spectrum and knockout parameters. Those current documents clarify semantics; the stored parameters from our pinned build establish what was actually executed.

## Next steps evaluated against these findings

### 1. Highest priority: locate MS1 detection and mapping losses

Use one representative injection from each mixture for a diagnostic baseline with the current scientific settings. Preserve converted/prepared mzML, rescored idXML, effective parameter files and complete MS1 logs. Measure successive counts for detected patterns, mapped IDs, label conflicts, retained quantitative peptides, group-compatible peptides and protein support.

Choose a small set of abundant identified peptides that are absent from the quantitative outputs. Inspect their labeled precursor isotope envelopes and chromatograms, including preservation of signal through conversion/peak picking. Distinguish absent/weak channel signal, detection rejection and mapping rejection before changing thresholds. In particular, evaluate the 5-second RT mapping tolerance against actual feature/ID offsets rather than loosening it blindly.

**Why first:** identification remains broad while only 8–15% of identified sequence strings reach features. Support replay leaves most of the publication gap unexplained. The old HeLa work/cache was removed, and no corresponding intermediate mzML/idXML were found in the inspected data directories, so a new diagnostic run may be needed; the PXD001101 cache does not restore these HeLa tasks.

### 2. Controlled MS1 sensitivity comparisons with fixed identifications

Once diagnostic inputs are retained, compare one change per invocation: missing-channel pattern handling (`knock_out`), neighboring-spectrum support (`rt_band`), or an intensity/isotope threshold justified by the inspected signals. Verify that the pinned workflow/resolver correctly handles partial patterns and label identities before interpreting a knockout-mode output. A singlet cannot supply a measured pairwise ratio.

For each comparison report newly quantified entities, missing-channel structure, log2 ratio error, support, technical completeness and matched precision. Evaluate newly admitted entities separately from the stable shared core. Scale to all six injections only after a representative change has an explained effect.

**Assessment:** higher priority than changing the search engine or all search inputs at once. Feature sensitivity and false-pattern risk must be evaluated together; greater coverage alone is not acceptance.

### 3. Resolve reference-table provenance and peptide-level matching

Retrieve the deposited MaxQuant archive when network access permits and identify `proteinGroups.txt`, `evidence.txt` and associated configuration/summary files. Establish which raw/normalized ratio columns generated the supplementary DDA table. Match peptide/charge/modification/run evidence and distinguish identified, matched-between-runs and requantified measurements. Select shared high-signal peptides for envelope inspection.

**Assessment:** essential to separate sample/acquisition effects from extraction/selection effects and to estimate MBR/requantification contributions. Current protein-group agreement does not establish identical supporting peptide evidence.

### 4. Treat support and protein assignment as explicit analysis choices

Retain two-peptide support as the documented baseline while evaluating the single-peptide population separately. The offline replay already quantifies its maximum gain on existing features. Assess ambiguous/razor peptide routing using the intended inference semantics and matched peptide evidence before adopting a different group policy.

**Assessment:** useful and relatively bounded, but support alone closes only 9–16% of the current H/L coverage gap. Neither changing the default to zero nor assigning shared peptides arbitrarily will explain the missing features.

### 5. Correct search provenance, then make an isolated reference-matched comparison

Use the corrected Trypsin/P metadata and freeze the chosen target/contaminant FASTA with clear provenance. Verify the historical database's identity rather than equating a matching filename with the original file. Keep the benchmark-specific modification set supported by the deposited XML. Record engine-specific fragment/recalibration choices.

**Assessment:** appropriate for a final comparable benchmark, but lower diagnostic priority than MS1 gates. Broad identification and the small observed before-proline class do not predict that digestion/database changes will recover several thousand missing quantitative groups by themselves. Keep identifications fixed for the preceding MS1 comparisons so those experiments remain interpretable.

### 6. Reassess MBR and reproducibility after the preceding checks

Test MBR as a separate completeness experiment with unchanged detection/ratio settings, avoiding pooling distinct mixtures as interchangeable biological samples. Verify feature-ID transfer and false-match control. Establish deterministic paired input ordering and acceptable numerical tolerances for repeated executions. Then freeze the six-file result and compact regression summaries.

**Assessment:** useful subsequent work. MBR can transfer identifications to eligible features; it should not be assumed to recover every physically undetectable weak channel or reproduce MaxQuant requantification. QPX consumer/schema validation is a separate export task and is not the leading explanation for the current quantitative losses.
